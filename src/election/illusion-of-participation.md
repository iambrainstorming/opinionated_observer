# The Illusion of Participation: Why the Opposition’s Argument Against Boycotting Elections is Deeply Flawed

Date: 04-10-2026

It is an open secret in the public consciousness that elections in India have devolved into a completely rigged game. The machinery of voting has been compromised, and everyone knows it. Yet, faced with this reality, the political opposition has chosen to participate, offering a series of deeply flawed justifications for their refusal to boycott the polls. 

According to the opposition’s current logic, boycotting the elections is a strategic blunder. "It boomerangs because the ruling party gets 90% of the seats," one Opposition leader argued. Another rued that a boycott would amount to "yielding space to the political forces they want to defeat." 

But this reasoning is not just pessimistic; it is fundamentally incorrect. By clinging to a rigged electoral process, the opposition is not saving democracy; they are merely providing a fig leaf of legitimacy for a broken system.

## The Logical Inconsistency of "Political Survival"

The contradiction at the heart of the opposition’s strategy was sharply highlighted on September 25 by Abhijeet Dipke, the founder of the Cockroach Janta Party political campaign. Dipke called on Opposition parties to boycott the polls if they truly do not trust the Election Commission to hold free and fair elections. 

Dipke contended that the opposition’s stance is a glaring logical inconsistency: why are they actively participating in an exercise they themselves contend is rigged? As Dipke rightly pointed out, their participation is not driven by democratic duty, but merely for their own "political survival." When politicians prioritize their parliamentary seats over the integrity of the electoral process, they cease to be an opposition and become mere participants in a theatrical farce.

## Debunking the "Boomerang" and "Yielding Space" Myths

The opposition’s fear that a boycott will allow the ruling party to sweep 90% of the seats misunderstands the nature of political legitimacy. A 90% victory in a boycotted election is not a mandate; it is a pyrrhic victory that exposes the regime’s complete lack of popular consent. It strips the ruling party of the democratic shield they use to justify authoritarian overreach on the global and domestic stage.

Furthermore, the argument that boycotting means "yielding space" is entirely false. You do not yield space by playing a game where the dice are loaded; you reclaim space by flipping the board. 

## Redefining Boycott: From Passive Withdrawal to Active Mobilization

The opposition’s flawed argument relies on a passive definition of boycotting. Boycotting doesn't mean just staying home and doing nothing. A true electoral boycott is an active, aggressive campaign of civil disobedience. 

If the opposition truly believes the elections are rigged, they need to protest, mobilize, and organize 30-40% of their supporters to physically and politically disrupt the process. We have about 65% of voters who are not supporters of the BJP. If elections are still conducted after a boycott call, these mobilized masses must *gherao* polling booths. Through sheer mass protest and organized disruption, they can create enough pressure to stop the election dates from even being declared. 

## The Unstoppable Force of Nonviolent Mass Resistance

The opposition vastly underestimates the power of the masses. As legal experts and commentators have noted, India’s population is so vast that if just 1% of the people come out for a protest, it creates an unstoppable human tide. In the words of one lawyer, this one percent, walking together in solidarity, will crush the fascist forces through the sheer, overwhelming weight of their numbers—not even the corpse of authoritarianism will be found.

The tactics for this mass mobilization are clear and within reach: sit for hunger strikes in every single locality, and call for a nationwide *India Bandh*. 

The opposition also underestimates the youth of this country. Our youngsters do not fear the police. What will the police do? Beat them? Detain them? The youth are fearless enough to tolerate it, and they will do so with strict nonviolence. This is Gandhi’s country. The ideological battle is clear, and the followers of Godse will ultimately be defeated by the resilient, nonviolent spirit of the masses.

## Elections as a Social Contract

To understand why a boycott is necessary, one must understand what an election actually is. An election is not merely a mechanical process of dropping paper into a box; it is a social construct, a social contract between the state and the citizenry. 

Like all social constructs, it relies entirely on collective belief. If the people and their representatives no longer believe in the fairness of the process, the construct automatically collapses. You cannot force a social contract to function through administrative fiat when the underlying trust has been annihilated.

## The Shadow Agenda: Secret Collaboration and Post-Election Defections

If "political survival" is the stated goal, one must ask: what is the real plan behind participating in these elections? Is the opposition’s true strategy to merely contest the polls, secretly collaborate with the fascist government, and then conveniently switch sides after the results are declared?

This is not mere speculation; it is a documented, recurring pattern of behavior. This is exactly what they keep doing. [Since 2022, a staggering 111 MPs and MLAs have changed parties after winning their elections](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/111-sitting-mps-and-mlas-changed-their-political-parties-after-being-elected-adr), and many more are reportedly waiting in the wings to switch allegiances. 

This revolving door of defections proves that for many in the opposition, the election is not a battle of ideologies, but a mere audition for power. By participating in a rigged system, they are not fighting authoritarianism; they are keeping their resumes updated for a future merger or defection. This cynical maneuvering betrays the voters who cast their ballots in good faith, only to see their mandates hijacked by opportunistic politicians.

## Conclusion: The Danger of Legitimizing the Rigged Game

By choosing to participate in a rigged election, the opposition is doing the ruling party’s work for them. They are taking an illegitimate exercise and making it look legitimate simply by showing up. Worse, they are asking the voters to join them in this charade, asking the public to invest their time, hope, and votes into a process the opposition itself knows will not result in a fair victory.

The opposition’s argument against boycotting is a surrender disguised as strategy. If they do not trust the Election Commission, they must boycott. If they want to defeat the forces they oppose, they must stop playing by rules that have been entirely discarded by the ruling party. True opposition requires the courage to break the illusion, mobilize the masses, and demand a return to genuine democracy, rather than settling for survival in a rigged game.
