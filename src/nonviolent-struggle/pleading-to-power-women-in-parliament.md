# From Pleading to Power: Why Indian Women Must Demand 50% Parliamentary Parity

Date: 30-09-2026

The cycle is exhausting, and the emotional toll is immeasurable. Recently, a viral video exposed the brutal harassment and molestation of two teenagers at night in Jamui, Bihar. Shortly after, the nation was horrified by the alleged gang rape of a 17-year-old girl in a Delhi park by men posing as police officers. In response, women in Bihar and across the country have once again taken to the streets. 

But a closer look at these protests reveals a deeply troubling pattern: women are crying out for safety, pleading with a male-centric Parliament and state legislatures to protect them. It is the same protest, rinsed and repeated every six months. The heartbreaking truth is that begging for safety from the very patriarchal structures that enable it is a failing strategy. It is time for a fundamental shift. Indian women must stop asking for safety and start demanding 50% parliamentary parity. 

## The Reality of a Broken System

To understand why the current approach is failing, one must look at the cold, scientific reality of the data. The National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) reports an average of 80 to 86 rapes *daily* in India. This is not a series of isolated anomalies; it is a statistical indicator of systemic collapse. 

Furthermore, the danger is not just outside the home; it is woven into the domestic fabric. The National Family Health Survey-5 (NFHS-5, 2019–2021) reveals that 29.3% of ever-married women aged 18–49 have experienced physical or sexual violence by their spouses, with 32% reporting physical, sexual, or emotional violence in their lifetime. 

From a sociological and criminological perspective, when a system consistently produces these outcomes over decades, the system itself is the variable that must change. A legislature dominated by men lacks the lived experience required to comprehend the pervasive, everyday nature of this violence. You cannot solve a crisis you do not personally fear.


## The Illusion of Male-Centric Allyship

The political landscape further proves the futility of relying on male lawmakers for women’s safety. Rhetoric often masks reality. For example, while prominent national leaders like Rahul Gandhi run campaigns with slogans to "Smash patriarchy," the ground reality tells a different story. Critics rightly point out that in states like [Karnataka, under a Congress-led government, there have been cabinets formed with zero women](https://theprint.in/politics/after-no-women-karnataka-cabinet-now-state-congresss-new-district-presidents-are-all-men/3040539/). 

This is the ultimate empirical evidence of the problem: when men hold 100% of the executive and legislative power, women’s safety remains a peripheral talking point, not a governing priority. Why should women continue to beg male-centric MPs and MLAs to provide them safety, when the track record shows that male-dominated bodies consistently fail to prioritize it? 

## Why 50% Parity is the Only Logical Solution

It is no longer enough to cry out, "Give us safety." Safety cannot be gifted as a favor by those who do not experience the threat. Women must demand 50% reservation in Parliament, not as a plea, but as a non-negotiable requirement for functional governance. 

Here is why this shift is imperative:
1. **From Petitioners to Architects**: With 50% of the seats, women transition from being passive recipients of male-drafted laws to active architects of their own security. Policies regarding street lighting, public transport safety, workplace harassment, and fast-track courts, gender equal curriculum will be designed by those who actually need to use these spaces.
2. **Structural Accountability**: When women hold half the power, they hold half the accountability. The burden of solving gender-based violence will no longer fall on a system that historically ignores it. 
3. **Breaking the Cycle**: The "rinse and repeat" cycle of protesting every six months only works if the goal is to raise awareness. Awareness has been achieved. The data is public. The videos are viral. The next step is not more awareness; it is the redistribution of power.

## Reclaiming Space Through Non-Violent Power

The roads, the parks, and the workspaces belong to women just as much as they belong to men. If these spaces are not safe, it is because the legislature does not reflect the people who walk them. 

Women possess the demographic weight and the moral authority to change this. If the current system refuses to make these spaces safe, 50% of the population is powerful enough to force the change through sustained, strategic, non-violent protest. 

The time for crying out to a patriarchal Parliament is over. Begging has not stopped the violence in Jamui, and it has not secured the parks of Delhi. It is time to take away a fair share of the power. It is time to demand 50% parity, take accountability, and legislate safety from a position of strength, not at the mercy of men.
